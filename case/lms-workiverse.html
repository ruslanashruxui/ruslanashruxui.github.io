03.1 — FROM RESEARCH TO DECISIONS

What I saw — and what came out of it

I didn’t copy competitors. I looked for patterns they all share and gaps nobody covers — and turned every observation into a product decision.

TalentLMS

Corporate training and compliance; Branches as separate portals

LearnWorlds

Online school + B2B; the widest range of activity types

Thinkific

Course creators: sales, subscriptions, B2B seats

Moodle

Classic LMS; Workplace adds tenants, programs, recertification

Circle

Learning through community, events and discussion

WHAT I SAWWHAT I DECIDED
01

Every platform splits people into admin, author and learner — with variations like Seat Manager or Tenant Admin.

TalentLMS · LearnWorlds · Thinkific · Moodle · Circle
→

Three roles in the LMS — with Organisation Admin designed in the most depth, because that’s who sets up, assigns and controls training.

02

Different audiences live in separate portals: Branches in TalentLMS, tenants in Moodle Workplace.

TalentLMS · Moodle
→

White-label as the foundation: every organisation or training company gets its own branded space.

03

Learning paths always come with completion rules, while gamification (badges, levels, leaderboards) lives separately from courses.

TalentLMS · Moodle
→

Two path types: Standard with a minimum share of required items, and Challenge with a day-by-day plan and gamification.

04

Compliance isn’t “completed a course” — it’s deadlines, certificate expiry and recertification.

Moodle Workplace · TalentLMS
→

A dedicated Compliance & Certificates module: training rules with deadlines, expiry tracking, reissuing and revoking certificates.

05

Only one platform out of five has a public course marketplace.

TalentLMS
→

The marketplace as a differentiator: organisations buy ready-made courses or publish their own for others.

06.3 — KEY DECISIONS

What I chose — and why

DECISION 01

AI starts with the outline

In Phase 1, AI generates the course structure — sections and lessons. Full lesson text moved to Phase 2: a weak AI feature is worse than none.

DECISION 02

AI where writing is hardest

Title, description and cover are among the biggest blockers when starting a course, so AI help there came first.

DECISION 03

Autosave from minute one

“Saved just now” in the header. If an admin spends two hours on a course, a crashed browser shouldn’t cost them anything.

DECISION 04

Anchor bar for long lessons

Chips with block types under the lesson title scroll to each block — essential once a lesson has 10+ blocks.

07 — GAMIFIED MODE

Duolingo-style learning

The client wanted a Duolingo-style format. I designed it as a separate course format with its own builder: a lesson is a path of small steps — a question, a flashcard or an interaction — and each step earns XP.

DECISION

A separate format, not a toggle

Micro-steps, XP and instant feedback need their own builder, not extra settings on a classic course.

09 — MONETISATION

Free should feel like a preview, not a broken product

The LMS is sold on Free, Growth and Enterprise plans. I defined what is locked on Free in every module and how a lock looks, so admins understand the limit and their next step.

THE RULE

Learning actions need Growth; organisational actions stay free

On Free, assigning learning paths, analytics, compliance and certificates are locked. Company structure, communication and inviting users stay open.

PATTERN 01

Locked action

Assign learning pathAvailable on Growth plan · Upgrade →

PATTERN 02

PATTERN 03

Locked page

Assignments

Assign courses and learning paths to your team with due dates and reminders. Available on Growth plan.

View plans →

DECISION 01

Lock the whole page when it would be empty

Assignments and Compliance depend on paths and assignments that don’t exist on Free. An empty page full of locked buttons is worse than one clear message.

DECISION 02

The catalog stays open

On Free, admins browse, filter and read courses but can’t buy or assign them — a natural paywall without a technical block.

DECISION 03

Limits explain themselves

“You’ve reached the Free plan limit of 10 users. Upgrade to Growth to add more →” — instead of a button that silently does nothing.

DECISION 04

One block in the sidebar

No plan — “View plans”; tokens below 20% — “Buy tokens”; all good — the block disappears.

10 — REFLECTION

What this project taught me

Research is the foundation of product decisions

Deep competitor analysis helped me not only define the functionality but also argue for decisions, priorities and the boundaries of the product.

Every decision has its “why”

Documenting the logic and reasons behind design decisions made it easier to align on requirements, communicate with the team and adapt to change.

System first, interface second

Working inside an ecosystem showed how important it is to define roles, the links between modules and the business logic first — and only then design screens.

AI speeds up the process, but doesn’t replace thinking

Figma Make helped me test UX hypotheses and build complex flows faster. At the same time, the quality of the result depended on clear requirements and product decisions.
